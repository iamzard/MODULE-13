The big idea

Technology can change jobs, not just remove them.

AI is technology that can do things like understand language, find patterns, or make predictions. Automation means using technology to do tasks with less human help. What happens depends on which tasks change and how people change the way they work.

For example: AI drafts replies to common customer questions. A worker checks each reply before sending it.
One thing to remember: AI is what the technology can do. Automation is how it does a task. They can be used together, but not every automated task uses AI, and AI doesn’t always do the task by itself.

02 · AI in schools

AI can help students, but teachers still matter.

AI tools can give students practice, language help, and feedback. But they work best when they support what students need to learn, are easy to use, and are checked by a teacher. Student information also needs to stay private.

For example: A teacher sees that a student is having trouble with fractions. They use that information to choose extra practice that fits the student's needs.
Remember: Check AI-made materials, keep student information private, and be clear about when AI is allowed. Make sure students understand the work themselves.

03 · AI at work

AI should fit the job it is used for.

At work, AI can sort requests, sum up documents, write a first draft, or find patterns in information. It works better when the information is good and people know how to check its answers. Privacy matters too.

For example: AI writes a first draft of meeting notes and a to-do list. The meeting leader checks the notes, adds any missing details, and makes sure each task has an owner and a deadline.
Remember: Treat AI's answer as a draft. Check it, and don't use it instead of your own professional judgment.

04 · Automation

Let technology do routine tasks, but plan for problems.

Automation follows set steps to do a task. This can make work faster and more consistent, but it may not know what to do when something unusual happens. Automation can use AI, but it doesn't have to.

For example: When someone buys an item online, the system sends a receipt, updates the stock count, and lets the store know when it is time to order more.
Before you automate: Make sure the process works well first. Decide who will check for problems and fix them if something goes wrong.

05 · Robots

Robots can do tasks in the real world.

Robots use machines, sensors, and software to move or respond to things around them. Some follow the same set of steps every time. Others use AI to respond to changes.

For example: Robots move boxes around a warehouse. Workers keep things safe, look after the robots, and handle tasks that need a person.
Remember: A robot doesn't have to use AI. People still need clear safety rules, and a way to stop the robot if there's a problem.

06 · People working with AI

People and AI can do different parts of a job.

AI can search through lots of information and find patterns. People can add context, make decisions, and take responsibility. We need to choose carefully which tasks go to AI and which stay with people.

For example: AI sorts a lot of public comments into groups. A worker checks that the groups make sense and that different people's views are represented fairly.
AI: handles lots of informationPeople: add context and checkTogether: better results

07 · AI can help with decisions

People should still be responsible for important decisions.

AI can offer a suggestion, but it may be wrong or miss important details. People checking the answer need enough time, knowledge, and authority to question it.

For example: A computer system points out which patients may need follow-up. A doctor checks the information and decides what is best for each patient.

08 · Learning new skills

People can learn more for their job, or train for a new one.

Upskilling means learning more skills for the job you already have. Reskilling means learning skills for a different kind of job. Good training should lead to real job opportunities.

For example: A store worker learns to use inventory data in their current job. That's upskilling. If they train for a different job in tech support, that's reskilling.

09 · Jobs in the future

AI can change jobs in different ways.

Technology can take over some tasks, help people with others, and create new tasks. The effect on jobs depends on what customers need and how employers change the work. Different workers and communities may be affected in different ways.

For example: Accounting software can do some data entry. Accountants may spend more time checking unusual cases, reading reports, and helping clients. It depends on how the employer changes the job.
One thing to remember: Look at how many jobs there are, what those jobs are like, and how hard it is for workers to move to a new job. These can have different answers.

A closer look · Tasks, not job titles

A task could change without a whole job going away.

Most jobs are made up of many tasks. AI might be able to do one task, but that doesn't mean a company will use it or remove the job. Cost, safety, rules, and people's choices all matter.

For example: Software can enter routine transactions, while accountants still handle unusual cases and give advice. The job changes, but what happens to jobs depends on what the company needs and decides.

A closer look · Productivity and who benefits

More work done doesn't mean everyone benefits.

Automation can do some tasks and help people with others. It may also lower costs or create new work. But that doesn't guarantee more jobs or better pay. It matters who owns the technology, who makes decisions, and how the gains are shared.

For example: AI may help a team serve more clients. The company could improve service and pay, serve more people without changing pay, or cut jobs. The result depends on the choices the company makes.

A closer look · What work is like

Faster work isn't always better work.

Some companies use computer systems to give out work and track how people do it. This can help organize work, but unclear rules can make work harder or unfair. Good jobs also need to be safe and fair, and workers should be able to speak up.

For example: A delivery app may help drivers find routes faster. Drivers still need safe routes, clear rules, and a fair way to explain a delay or challenge a wrong rating.

A closer look · Changing how a company works

Adding AI means changing the way work is done.

Buying AI software isn't enough. Workers need good tools, training, and clear rules. If the system doesn't fit the job, people may spend more time checking mistakes or have less say in their work.

For example: Before a hospital starts using AI to write notes, it checks that the tool saves doctors time, doesn't make them do more corrections, and keeps patient information private.
A good way to start: Check how things work now. Try the tool with workers, see what goes well and what doesn't, make changes, and keep a backup plan.

A closer look · Learning about AI

Students need to know their subject and how to check AI.

Being AI-smart means knowing what AI can and can't do, checking its answers, keeping information safe, and being honest about using it. Students need to know their subject so they can spot answers that sound right but are wrong.

For example: Students use AI to plan an essay, check its ideas against their readings, and explain what they changed. They also show that they understand their own ideas.
Keep it fair: Make the rules clear and give students the help and access they need. (UNESCO, 2023)

10 · Using AI responsibly

We need to keep checking AI for problems.

AI can cause problems because of the information it uses, the way it is built, or how people use it. Before and after using a system, we should think about who could be affected, check for harm, and decide who is responsible.

Fairness

Check that the system doesn't treat groups of people unfairly.

Privacy

Only collect the information that's needed, and keep it safe.

Responsibility

Decide who is in charge, check how the system works, and fix problems.

Clear information

Explain what the system can and can't do, and how it affects decisions.

For example: A company checks that its hiring system is fair, uses only the information it needs, tells applicants how AI is used, and lets a person review or challenge a decision.

Let’s talk · Question 1 of 4

Will AI take people's jobs or help them?

AI may do some tasks, help with others, and change what workers do. What happens depends on the kind of AI, the choices a company makes, and how much time passes.

Let’s discuss: Should a company use AI mainly to save money, or to make work and service better? How can we tell if workers are benefiting too? We can look at pay, workload, job security, and whether workers get a say.
AI being able to do a task, a company choosing to use it, and jobs actually changing are three different things.

Let’s talk · Question 2 of 4

What can people do well when working with AI?

There may not be one skill that only people can do in every situation. But people bring experience, good judgment, care, and the ability to understand what is happening around them. AI may change how these skills are used.

For example: A customer-service worker listens to a difficult complaint, finds a fair solution, and helps the customer feel heard. They also need to notice when an AI answer is wrong or doesn't fit.

Let’s talk · Question 3 of 4

What should schools do about AI?

Schools need to help students learn their subjects and use AI wisely. They should make the rules clear, protect students' information, and give students work that shows how they think—not just the final answer.

For example: Students explain how they checked AI's ideas and what they changed. Then they use what they learned to solve a new problem without AI.
The rules may be different for different ages, subjects, and kinds of schoolwork.

Let’s talk · Question 4 of 4

How can companies help workers get ready?

Workers shouldn't have to figure everything out on their own. Companies can ask workers what they need, offer paid training, explain how AI affects decisions, and help people move into new jobs if needed.

For example: Before using AI to make staff schedules, a company tries it with workers, gives them training, checks that the schedules are fair, and makes it easy to report mistakes.

To wrap up

We all have a say in how work changes.

AI is powerful, but we must remember three important things. First, we should use AI carefully because it can make mistakes. Second, when companies use AI to replace or change certain jobs, they should also help workers learn new skills. Lastly, humans must remain responsible for the decisions and actions involving AI. We should not blame everything on technology.

The main lesson: AI should make our lives and work better, but it should never remove human responsibility, fairness, and good judgment.
